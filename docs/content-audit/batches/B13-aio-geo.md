Theses as read:
- 1: GEO optimises for being *cited*, not *ranked*; it sits on top of technical SEO and needs a new metric (citation share, AVS).
- 2: Being retrieved ≠ being read — rerank and grounding decide who gets cited, so write at chunk level with groundable facts.
- 3: GEO is four work-layers with distinct owners; do Layer 1 (crawler access) first because it is 0/1.
- 4: Eight copy-paste steps take a site from Level 0/1 to Level 2 with CI-verifiable acceptance.
- 5: AI exposure is invisible in GA4, so measure proactively with a prompt set — and the biggest wins turn out to be bug fixes, not content.
- 6: In a large enterprise the GEO bottleneck is governance (access-layer archaeology, legal pre-definition), not technique.
- 7: E-commerce GEO is a data-rendering problem, not a content-production problem; facet pages are the new battleground.
- 8: A three-person team wins by abandoning category-discovery queries for specific-problem pages, honest comparison pages and open source.
- 9: Paywalled content needs an open/preview/closed three-layer design; the public summary is the best trial lesson.
- 10: Internal RAG failures are isomorphic to public GEO; the missing piece is a self-built authority signal (status/owner/reviewed_at).
- 11: Most GEO market data is written by GEO sellers; the honest pitch is "small, high-quality, growing", and the moat is benchmark data.
- 12: GEO is mostly SEO — sell it as three new acceptance criteria, gated by a four-dimension client score.
- 13: Run the diagnosis live in the sales meeting; write contract KPIs as deliverables, never as a citation rate.
- 14: The ceiling differs by industry (dev tools 35–50 %, medical 10–20 %); pick one or two industries and go deep.
- 15: Buy time (AI Overview scraping, reports), never judgment; the benchmark database is the only non-copyable asset.
- 16: A consultancy breaks between clients 6 and 9; track hours-per-client, automate the monthly report, push content work back to the client.
